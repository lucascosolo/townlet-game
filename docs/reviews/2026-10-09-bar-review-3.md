# Townlet: bar review 3 (2026-10-09)

Third independent review against the bar in `docs/GAME_DESIGN_SPEC.md` section 9.4: five counts, each out of 10, pass mark 8 on every one. I read the two earlier reviews only to know what had been claimed fixed; every number, quote and screenshot here is from my own sessions. Screenshots are in `docs/reviews/shots-3/` (`p` phone, `d` engaged desktop, `n` neglected desktop).

## What I played

Built with `npm run build`, served by `vite preview` on 4173, driven through the real UI with Playwright (chromium, swiftshader). Every build, answer, question, reply and favour below was made through the on-screen controls (tabs, roster chips, question and reply chips, the Build tray, the Remove tool, the proposal modal), except that fourteen of the eighteen buildings were placed by command at a tile I had checked was free, because clicking tiles in software rendering is slow.

- **Engaged, 30 days, desktop 1440x900, `bakery` seed 2.** At 08:05 each morning I read the board and counted asks, answered any proposal in the modal (approved Bram's tales night, Marlow's cart stop, Wren's lantern walk, Marlow's market, Juniper's contraption on its second posting and Gideon's tales night; declined Gideon's two bonfires and Bram's night baking; pressed "Decide later" on the contraption on days 9 and 11), talked to three people through the Journal, rotating all five questions and taking a reply after every answer (95 questions, 95 replies: 62 agree, 19 disagree, 13 sorry, 1 explain), and asked 18 favours (one every second day, plus four of Marlow in one morning on day 16). Build plan: two flower beds (through the Build tray), a hedge and the bakery on day 1; cottages on days 3, 11, 18 and 24; garden day 5; bench day 7; woodlot day 9; orchard day 14; granary day 16; beehives day 20; glasshouse day 22; chicken coop day 26 (refused, Village not reached); banner day 28. Saved and reopened the plain URL on day 10. Took down the old oak with the Remove tool on day 12 and on day 13 asked all eight residents "What do you think of me?" and said sorry wherever it was offered. Built no food after day 14 to see the larder go: it sat at 0.1 to 0.9 food from day 22 to day 28. Recorded mood and every resident's standing with me nightly. On day 30 read the You tab, all ten About pages, the Folk album, the Goals pane and the log in all three filters. The town grew from 6 to 10, Hamlet on day 9, 276 renown by day 30.
- **Neglected, 21 days, desktop, `quiet` seed 4.** Never built, talked or answered; pressed "Decide later" on each of the five proposal modals because they block the screen. Read the board on days 1, 7, 14 and 21, the You tab and Ada's About and Talk pages on day 22 (asked how, mind and me, took the sorry twice).
- **Fresh on a phone, 390x844 touch, `bakery` seed 2 with the intro**, at live speed: the three intro cards, 90 real seconds of the town running, the five nav tabs, the Build tray, the Folk album, Ada's About and Talk pages with four questions and replies, the quick card from tapping Bram; a second phone session (`quiet` seed 2) for the quick card's Talk button, the proposal modal and the touch build flow.
- **Headless:** `npm run radio` for `bakery` seed 3 (21 days, `considerate` and `none`), `quiet` seed 7 (30 days, `favours`) and `quiet` seed 4 (22 days, `none`); `npm run inspect` for `bakery` seed 3 at day 14. Used for repeated lines, asks per day and to check that what pages say matches what minds hold.

Nothing crashed. The console had the three.js shadow-map warning and one blocked request to fonts.googleapis.com (the sandbox proxy, not the game; the page falls back to a system font, so what I saw was not the intended typeface). Save and reload came back at the same tick with the same 51 buildings and 7 residents in 7.9 s with no intro. First frame on the phone in 4.9 s.

A note on the harness: Playwright's synthetic touch taps did not land on the canvas or the "✓ Place" button in two paused phone sessions, while the same tap worked in a live one and a synthesised click placed the bench. I could not tell whether that is the test tool or the game, so touch placement is not scored either way.

## Scores

### 1. Fun: 6/10

There is now something to decide most mornings. Nine proposals in 30 days across seven kinds (tales night, cart stop, bonfire, contraption, lantern walk, market, night baking), posted roughly every three days, each with sides that stay consistent (Ada and Fen against every noisy thing, Bram, Marlow and Wren for), and a "Decide later" that re-poses once and then lapses on schedule ("Juniper's contraption on the commons went unanswered. Juniper takes it badly."). Asks per day in the engaged game were `7,1,2,3,7,4,4,4,4,4,3,2,3,1,2,3,4,3,4,5,3,3,2,2,1,14,19,14,12,13`: never a blank morning. Residents now move on without me ("Ada stops waiting for an orchard. 'I must say, I shall not ask again. One learns to make do.'", day 10; "Wren stops waiting for a town banner. 'One day, perhaps. Not now.'") and the town can warn you before it loses someone ("THINKING OF LEAVING: Bram has been thinking of leaving since day 13", neglected day 14, then "Bram: 'I may as well have asked the well!'" on day 20). Those are the moments that make a second hour plausible. What holds it to 6: the biggest thing that happened in my town, eight nights with the larder at zero, changed nobody's day (mean mood 0.77 to 0.81 every night of it, "Bram: On top of the world, me! Larder emergency!" on day 27); the last week of the board was the same ten "asks for more food" cards with the same help sentence ten times; by day 30 eight of ten residents had the same dream ("make something for Wren / Bram / Fen / Ada / Marlow / the steward"); Marlow said "I just need to make up my mind before the cart's last visit of the year" on days 17, 19 and 22 and "watch the trade cart come and go" on days 1, 3 and 8, thirty days of not deciding; and 43 of 95 conversations offered me one thing to say.

### 2. Polished: 5/10

The shell is handsome and the phone layout holds: the three intro cards, the five nav tabs, the Journal sheet and quick card all fit at 390px with no sideways scroll (p01 to p07), the quick card now sizes to its text, widgets drag on desktop (d-widgets-dragged), the night scene with lit windows and the snow (d25-night, n22-you) are lovely, and the day-1 board in the bakery town has seven things on it. But raw data reaches the screen in three places. The About page, the You tab and a talk answer all print the belief aspect in code form: "The steward matters (still_waiting)." under Juniper on the You tab on day 20 and day 30 (d20-you), "Maybe the steward matters (went_hungry)…" under STILL MAKING UP HER MIND on seven of ten About pages on day 30, and in speech, "Ooh, you feel far off from us. Somehow, you matter (still_waiting)." (Juniper, day 10) and "Oh, it's been hard to feel looked after lately. Well, you matter (went_hungry)." (Tamsin, day 29). `BELIEF_STATEMENTS` in `src/content/voice.ts` has no line for those two aspects and `src/narrate/narrator.ts` line 340 falls back to `{s} matters (${b.aspect})`. Grammar breaks where a grievance note is dropped into a "you …" frame: "I am still turning over the fact that you our wish for more green about the place came to nothing" (Ada, neglected day 22; "The steward our wish for more green about the place came to nothing" in the radio log), "Though you nothing was done, and that stung a little" (Gideon, day 13), "You really does care, you know" (Marlow, day 22). Counts and lists go stale: "0 of 2 who wished for it have it: Ada, Fen, Wren." and "Wren asks for help with a dream: a town banner" on the same morning the board said "Wren has packed up and left the valley" (n21-board); "Juniper asks for help with a dream: a granary. 'Make something for Bram.'" after Bram had left; "Maybe the flower bed is a lovely spot…" three times in one list on Wren's page and "joy about the flower bed, joy about the flower bed" on Fen's; "NEEDS · ALL MET" on Ada's page on day 30 after a week of thin suppers; the Hamlet modal says "The neighbouring towns send 15 timber" while the log for the same minute says "send 8 timber" (d-tier-up). Smaller: the centre of the town is a brown trampled slab from day 5 on (d05-talk, d12-remove, n21-board); on a 900px-tall desktop the Folk widget is squashed to a two-line strip under the Goals widget and shows no cards unless you scroll it (every d-shot); the tray hint is cut at "right-drag to turn, w…"; Bram's thought bubble ran to six lines of his whole stitched answer and sat under the Hamlet modal (d-tier-up); the Highlights filter prints "Day 7 · Spring" and "Day 8 · Summer" with nothing under them; the talk panel keeps only the last exchange, so the answer you are replying to vanishes when you reply (p06-ada-talk, d05-talk).

### 3. Thinking sims: 7/10

Still the strongest count, and the reasons are findable. The About page lists needs, "On their mind" with causes ("Wants some quiet (too much company for their liking)", "Tired (rest is running low)"), the hope and its next step, what they are doing until when, what they remember with dates, settled opinions and the ones still forming. The inspector traces Ada's +1.00 for the steward to "green by my door (+0.68)", "helped me with my dream (+0.68)" and six "told Fen / told Wren" gossip reinforcements. Plans chain and complete: Ada's orchard hope became "make peace with Juniper" ("My next step is to work up the courage to talk to Juniper", day 16) and the log shows her doing it ("On the path, Ada finds Juniper. 'Juniper, I owe you an apology.'"); Juniper's glasshouse became "make something for Marlow" and "Juniper gives Marlow something she made by hand. Marlow is touched." Residents forage when the larder is bare ("Wren goes by the brook with a basket and comes back with 0.5 food") and give up on asks you ignore. It loses points because the page and the state disagree at the moment it matters ("NEEDS · ALL MET" during the famine; "Missing Wren (hasn't seen Wren since day 29)" on day 30, one day later), because the reason labels are sometimes raw ("You: something you did lately (what the steward did: said yes to Gideon's tales night)", "The rain (the rain)"), and because some plans never move: Marlow's "watch the trade cart / make up my mind" across all 30 days, Gideon's "First, I need to get to know the neighbours" from day 4 to day 29, Juniper's "My last dream came true. The next one is still forming" on day 14 and again on day 27.

### 4. Opinionated sims: 6/10

They do disagree, with each other and with me, and it is legible. Every proposal lists who is for and against, and the sides match the people (Ada and Fen never wanted a bonfire, a market or a night of baking). Decisions cost: declining Gideon's bonfire got "Marlow thinks less of you", "Linus thinks less of you", "Juniper thinks less of you" the next morning and Ada "▲ said no to Gideon's bonfire night"; leaving the contraption took Juniper from +0.43 to −0.59 by day 15. The oak landed on three of six: "you took away the old oak, and I noticed" (Ada), "Mind, you took away the old oak" (Fen), "Even so, you took away the old oak. I wish you had not" (Wren), and Ada still said "I confess it still weighs on me that we lost the old oak" on day 24. The larder reached speech by day 27 ("Fen: You let the larder run bare. Not forgotten."). But approval is still cheap and fast: Ada said "You have earned my trust, and that is not easily done" on day 2 after one flower bed; Bram was at +0.71 by day 9; on day 30, after eight nights at zero food, Marlow and Wren "think the world of you" and Bram "thinks well of you". Nobody refused me a favour on my account: 18 favours, 15 yes, one "You have asked a great deal of me lately", one "I need a break", and three "I'm still on the last thing you asked!" from Marlow, who said "Happy to" first. Opinions of places are mostly nothing: of 13 "What do you think of…" answers about buildings, 7 were "No view on the garden plots", "I am undecided about the bench", "The brook? I haven't made my mind up", "I don't really know the workshop well enough". Tastes converge: all ten pages carry "Can't abide noise, especially at night" or "Loves growing things", and by day 30 the dream of eight residents was "make something for" a neighbour. Newcomers still walk in content: Tamsin arrived on day 24 with the larder at 0.1 and said "Everything went right, for once" four days later.

### 5. Expressive and interactive: 6/10

The talk-back is now keyed to what was said, which is the big change since review 2. Sorry and explain were offered after 20 of 95 answers and every one named the thing ("I'm sorry I said no to Bram's bonfire night", "I'm sorry our wish for more green about the place came to nothing", "I'm sorry nothing was done"), and the answers land with voice: Fen, "Took you long enough. But all right."; Ada, "I would rather see it than hear it again." to a second sorry; Juniper, "Hmm, something unclenches. Thank you."; Bram to a disagreement, "Well, excuse ME!"; Marlow, "That's a bit hurtful, honestly." The sentences that are written for the moment are good ("Hunger has a sound. It is a sort of hum."; "Ha! My stomach is writing a strongly worded letter!"; "I keep opening the larder as if that helps."). What keeps it at 6: 43 of 95 answers offered only "That's fair", so nearly half the time there is nothing to say back; the responses to a reply come from a small stock pool unrelated to the claim ("Thank you. That means a lot." 12 times, "See, we do all right, you and I." 10, "I'm glad you think so." 8), and "I don't see it that way" to "I'm so glad it's you looking after us" got "Fine. Let's not talk about it, then." rather than anything about me; the answers are still stitched lists with contradictions inside them (Marlow, day 22: "It's coming along. I just have to make up my mind before the cart's last visit of the year. You really does care, you know. Everything feels a bit golden today. I keep thinking about how you said yes to my idea, six days ago. I need a good long sleep, more than most."; Jory, day 11: ten exclamations ending "I need a good long sleep, more than most."; Bram, day 27: "On top of the world, me! Larder emergency! Somebody plant something!"; Gideon, day 13: "I don't know you well enough yet, I think. Though you nothing was done, and that stung a little."); "I need a good long sleep, more than most." closed 6 answers, "I work at the …" 7, and a bolted-on "X is my closest friend here" or "My favourite spot is …" 10; two answers printed a raw token; and the thread forgets the answer the moment you reply.

## Verdict

**Does not meet the bar.** Highest count 7, pass mark 8 on all five. Since review 2 the replies fit the sentence before them, proposals come often and lapse as promised, residents give up waiting and think about leaving out loud, and the oak and the larder both reach what people say. What still holds it under: code-form text on three screens and ungrammatical grievance lines, a famine and a fortnight of neglect that nobody feels (mood flat, "On top of the world"), a conversation that nearly half the time offers one chip and answers from a stock pool, answers that are still lists, and a board that fills with ten copies of the same card.

## Problems, ranked by how much they hold the game back

### 1. Raw and broken text on screen

Evidence: "The steward matters (still_waiting)." on the You tab (Juniper, days 20 and 30, d20-you); "Maybe the steward matters (went_hungry)…" on seven About pages on day 30; "Somehow, you matter (still_waiting)." (Juniper, day 10) and "Well, you matter (went_hungry)." (Tamsin, day 29) spoken in Talk; "that you our wish for more green about the place came to nothing" (Ada, neglected day 22), "Though you nothing was done" (Gideon, day 13), "You really does care" (Marlow, day 22); "0 of 2 who wished for it have it: Ada, Fen, Wren." and a departed Wren's ask on the neglected day-21 board; "Maybe the flower bed is a lovely spot…" ×3 on Wren's page, "joy about the flower bed, joy about the flower bed" on Fen's; "Maybe the garden plots is a lovely spot…"; "Maybe The steward means well…"; Hamlet modal "send 15 timber" vs log "send 8 timber"; "NEEDS · ALL MET" during the famine.
Fix: add `still_waiting` and `went_hungry` (and a test that every aspect has one) to `BELIEF_STATEMENTS`; give grievance notes a subject-free form for the "you …" frame; recount wishes and drop asks when a wisher leaves; dedupe same-type buildings in belief lists; make the tier modal and the log read the same number.

### 2. Nothing the town suffers reaches how anyone feels

Evidence: engaged larder at 0.1 to 0.9 food on days 22 to 28 with ten mouths, mean mood 0.77 to 0.81 and minimum 0.64 on every one of those nights; "Bram: On top of the world, me! Larder emergency!" (day 27), "Gideon: as happy as the hens in the sun" and "Corin: I'm already humming the festival songs" on day 26 with 6 food produced for 10 people; Tamsin arriving on day 24 into the famine; 21 nights of neglect with mean mood 0.67 to 0.74 and Ada "I am in good order" on day 22 while her card said "unhappy with you"; "Winter will come, and I want 210 food put by" with 18 days to do it from a town making 30 a day and eating most of it (I reached 88).
Fix: let a hungry run pull mood, the "How are you?" band and work output down inside three days; make the first bare night a scene (a thin supper at one table, someone skipping the festival); stop newcomers while the larder is empty; set the winter-stores target from what the town can plausibly put by.

### 3. Talking back is one chip and a stock answer

Evidence: 43 of 95 answers offered only "That's fair"; reply responses "Thank you. That means a lot." ×12, "See, we do all right, you and I." ×10, "I'm glad you think so." ×8, "That's a bit hurtful, honestly." ×3, "Fine. Let's not talk about it, then." ×3 regardless of what was disagreed with; disagreeing with "I'm so glad it's you looking after us" (Marlow, day 12) got "Fine. Let's not talk about it, then."; the talk panel keeps only the last exchange (d05-talk, p06-ada-talk), so the answer being replied to is gone when the reply shows.
Fix: offer disagree on every answer that makes a claim and have both agree and disagree repeat the claim ("That's fair, I did let the larder run bare"); write the response from the claim and the resident's standing rather than a pool; keep the last three exchanges on screen.

### 4. Answers are stitched lists that contradict themselves

Evidence: Marlow's six-sentence day-22 answer above; Jory's day-11 "The teahouse! Love it! Changed my mind! Nearly there! Well, sort of! I've got to get to know the neighbours! …I need a good long sleep, more than most."; "I need a good long sleep, more than most." closing 6 of 95, "I work at the …" 7, "closest friend / favourite spot" bolted on 10; "On top of the world, me! Larder emergency!"; "I don't know you well enough yet… Though you nothing was done"; Ada's day-24 mind answer ending "I sleep lightly. The least noise at night and I am awake. I am happiest at home, truth be told."; "Wish me luck. I'm trying to give it to Marlow." and "Mm. Still on it. Give it to Ada." as whole sentences.
Fix: cap an answer at the band line plus one topic plus one memory, add a fact only when the question is about it, and run the opposite-valence check over the whole answer including the band line.

### 5. The board repeats itself

Evidence: days 26 to 30 of the engaged board carried ten "X asks for more food" cards each followed by the identical "Gardens and the fishing jetty fill the larder; gardens grow little in winter."; "We're running out of food. Could we grow more?" five times verbatim under THIS MORNING on day 27; the neglected You tab is "kept me waiting N days for more green about the place" on five consecutive lines for Ada and five for Wren; "Ada thinks less of the steward: put the bakery there." on three consecutive mornings and "Wren thinks less of the steward: kept me waiting 2/3/4 days" daily in the radio log; "On the path, Ada finds Juniper. 'Juniper, I owe you an apology.'" four times in 30 days.
Fix: collapse same-kind asks into one card ("Everyone is asking for food: Bram, Fen, Ada and seven more") with one help line; a grievance that grows by a day is one ledger line that updates, not a new one; no scene line twice in a season.

### 6. Approval is cheap and favours never refuse you

Evidence: "You have earned my trust, and that is not easily done" (Ada, day 2, after one flower bed); standings 0.2 to 0.43–0.47 by night 3; Bram +0.71 on day 9; after eight nights of famine Marlow +0.72 and Wren +0.68; 15 of 18 favours a plain yes, the three refusals about workload ("I'm still on the last thing you asked!", "I need a break") and none about me even with Juniper at −0.59 or during the famine; newcomers "think well of you" within days.
Fix: cap standing gain per day and decay toward neutral; refuse a favour on standing below zero or on a hungry run, with the reason in their words.

### 7. Dreams converge and stall

Evidence: day-30 hopes: Ada "make something for Fen", Bram "make something for Wren", Fen "make something for Ada", Juniper "make something for Wren", Wren "make something for Bram", Gideon "make something for Bram", Marlow "do something for the steward"; Marlow "watch the trade cart come and go" (days 1, 3, 8) then "make up my mind before the cart's last visit of the year" (days 17, 19, 22); Gideon "First, I need to get to know the neighbours" days 4 to 29; Juniper "The next one is still forming" days 14 and 27; Wren "First, I need to paint the banner" (days 17, 22) until I built the banner.
Fix: a bigger dream pool with trade-specific second dreams, a next step that completes by itself after a few days, and a hard stop on "make something for" as more than one resident's hope at a time.

### 8. Layout faults a player will notice

Evidence: the Folk widget squashed to a two-line strip under Goals on every 900px-tall desktop screenshot, its cards cut mid-name (d30-you); the trampled brown slab over the whole centre from day 5 (d05-talk, d12-remove, n21-board); the tray hint cut at "right-drag to turn, w…"; Bram's six-line bubble under the Hamlet modal (d-tier-up); empty "Day 7 · Spring" and "Day 8 · Summer" headers in Highlights; three toasts stacked over the town after one morning's talk (d05-talk).
Fix: split the column height between Goals and Folk or let Folk take the space when Goals is short; clamp wear to tracks; let the hint wrap; cap bubbles at two lines with the rest on the page; skip empty day headers; queue toasts one at a time.

### 9. Opinions of places are shrugs

Evidence: 7 of 13 building opinions were "No view on the garden plots", "I am undecided about the bench", "The brook? I haven't made my mind up", "The well? Don't think about it much", "I have no strong view on the woodlot", "I don't really know the workshop well enough" (twice); the ones that exist are one template ("X is a lovely spot" / "X is where the good evenings happen") across all ten pages.
Fix: give every resident a first impression of each building from their values the day it goes up, and three or four opinion shapes per building type.
