# Townlet: bar review 5 (2026-10-09)

This is the fifth independent review against the bar in `docs/GAME_DESIGN_SPEC.md` section 9.4: five counts, each scored out of 10, with a pass mark of 8 on every one. I read the four earlier reviews only to learn what had been claimed fixed and to match their shape. Every number, quote and screenshot here comes from my own sessions. Screenshots are in `docs/reviews/shots-5/`: `p` is the phone, `d` the engaged desktop game, `n` the neglected desktop game, and `o` a second, short desktop town I used for removing a loved place (explained below).

## What I played

I built the game with `npm run build`, served it with `vite preview` on port 4173, and drove the real UI with Playwright (chromium, swiftshader). Everything I did went through the on-screen controls: the Build tray and a click on a tile, the Remove tool, the proposal modal, the Journal roster, and the question, reply, picker and favour chips. I placed nothing by command.

- **Engaged, 30 days, desktop 1440x900, `bakery` seed 2.** Each day at 08:05 I read the board and answered any proposal modal on a fixed rotation. That came to 11 modals: 6 approved, 3 declined and 2 "Decide later", of which Bram's night baking then lapsed. I answered building asks near the asker's home and followed a building plan. 22 buildings went up through the tray: two bakeries, flower beds, hedges, benches, an orchard, a granary, a glasshouse, two banners, three cottages, a teahouse, a workshop and garden plots. Another 17 attempts were refused for lack of timber, all but one of them between days 2 and 13. Each noon I talked to three residents through the Journal, two questions each, rotating all five questions (how, mind, hope, me, and "What do you think of…" alternating person and place). After each answer I replied on a rotation of agree, disagree, sorry and explain, taking whatever was offered. I deliberately left every ninth answer without a reply, to test the chips. That came to 180 answers and 160 replies: 81 agree, 69 disagree, 6 sorry and 4 explain. On day 13 I asked everyone "What do you think of me?" and said sorry wherever I was offered the chance. I asked 33 favours, including four timber requests to Marlow in a row on day 16. On day 10 I saved and reopened the plain URL. On day 12 I took the old oak down with the Remove tool. From day 19 to day 27 I built no food, and on day 20 I removed the garden plots and a bakery to run the larder down. I recorded mood, food, granary and every resident's standing nightly. On day 30 I read the board, the You tab, all nine About pages, the Goals and Folk widgets, and the log in all three filters. The town grew from 6 to 9 people (Otto, Linus and Rosa), became a Hamlet on day 7 and a Village on day 22, and ended with 409 renown.
- **Neglected, 21 days, desktop, `quiet` seed 4.** I never built, talked or answered. The six proposal modals got "Decide later" because they block the screen. I read the board on days 1, 7, 14 and 21 and the You tab on day 21. On day 22 I read four About pages and asked Ada, Fen, Juniper and Marlow how, me, mind and hope, replying sorry or disagree where offered.
- **A loved place, desktop, `quiet` seed 4, days 1 to 14.** In the engaged town nobody turned out to be attached to the oak, so removing it tested nothing (see Thinking sims). So I ran a second, untouched town and approved every proposal. On day 12 I removed the oak with the Remove tool, and on day 14 I read four About pages and asked mind and me, offering sorry or explain where I could.
- **Fresh on a phone, 390x844 touch, `bakery` seed 7, with the intro**, at live speed. I went through the three intro cards, let 90 real seconds run, tapped all five nav tabs, opened Ada from the Folk album (About, then Talk with four questions and replies), tapped Bram on the map for the quick card, and placed a flower bed with the touch outline and "✓ Place".
- **Headless.** I ran `npm run radio` for `bakery` seed 6 (30 days, considerate), `quiet` seed 7 (30 days, none) and `quiet` seed 9 (30 days, favours), and `npm run inspect` for `bakery` seed 6 at day 14. I also wrote a short script that removes the oak on `quiet` seeds 1 to 5 with a considerate steward and counts the reactions.

**Crashes and console.** Nothing crashed and there were no page errors in any session. The console had the three.js warning "PCFSoftShadowMap has been removed. Using PCFShadowMap instead." and one failed request (ERR_CERT_AUTHORITY_INVALID). That is the sandbox proxy blocking the Google font, not the game, so the page fell back to a system typeface. Save and reload came back at the same tick (13860 to 13861) with the same 53 buildings and 6 residents in 7.4 s, and no intro. On the phone, `window.__townlet` was ready at 2.4 s and the loader was gone at 3.7 s. At 390 px the page never scrolled sideways on any screen I opened (scrollWidth 390 on all seven checks).

**Harness limits.**
- Software rendering runs at 1 to 2 fps, so I advanced time with `runTicks` between actions rather than watching it play. I read bubbles and toasts at moments I chose, not as a player would meet them.
- Questions, replies and building spots followed a fixed rotation, not taste.
- My building plan doubled the day-1 bakery ask, so the town had two bakeries. That is my script's mistake, not the game's.
- My attempt to starve the engaged town did not work. The granary already held 286 food when I took out the food buildings, so the larder sat at 0.8 to 12.8 food from day 22 to day 28 while nobody went hungry. I report that as I found it.
- On the phone, 90 seconds of live play reached only 13:11 on day 1, so no proposal arrived in that window.
- The preview server was stopped by a background time limit during the engaged run and restarted. The engaged page had already loaded, and its day-10 reload happened before the stop.

## Scores

### 1. Fun: 7/10

The month has decisions in it, and some of them now bite.
- **Proposals split the town along the residents' own tastes.** Eleven came in 30 days, each listing who is for and against: Ada and Fen against every noisy thing, Bram, Marlow and the young for it. Letting one lapse lands: "Bram's night of baking before the festival went unanswered. Bram takes it badly. Ada and Fen are glad. Juniper, Marlow, Wren and Linus are not pleased."
- **Over-asking has a cost.** After four timber requests in a row, Marlow said "I'm still on the last thing you asked!", and three days later "I've done a lot for you this week. I need a break."
- **Taking out the garden plots and a bakery had consequences.** Ada, Wren and Otto were left without work, the board grouped them ("Otto, Ada and Wren ask for a place to work: a garden plot"), and a season later: "The season ended without proper places to work. Ada, Wren and Otto had hoped for it."
- **Neglect has teeth.** In the neglected town, mean mood went from 0.69 on day 2 to 0.58 on day 21. Bram went to −1.00 standing, said "Maybe the road is calling, you know?" on day 12 and left on day 19. Wren reached −1.00 and was thinking of leaving. Ada gave up on me: "I have stopped expecting anything, which is a kind of peace."
- **The town surprised me.** Ada's dream moved from her sister's orchard to a gift for Wren to "make peace with Juniper", and on day 29 the log had "Ada finds Juniper. 'Honestly, allow me to apologise, Juniper. I was unkind.'" Fen started losing sleep to the bakery I built ("Another broken night for Fen, courtesy of the bakery.") and asked me about it in their own words: "Steward. The bakery. Nights. Can something be done?"

It stays at 7 because playing well stops mattering about halfway through:
- **Nothing in ordinary play moved the town's mood.** Engaged mean mood stayed between 0.72 and 0.82 on all 30 nights. That held through three declined proposals, a lapsed one, three jobless residents and a week with the larder under a day's meals.
- **By day 30, all six founders "think the world of you"** (standing 0.85 to 1.00), and nothing I did after day 10 dented that for more than a day.
- **The granary takes the edge off the larder.** It carried the town through my attempt to starve it, so the worry I was testing never appeared. Then at the turn of the year "Juniper shares out the last 196 food from the granary… The granary starts again from empty", and the next morning the board worried about 5 food for 9.
- **The first two weeks are starved of timber.** Seventeen builds were refused, most of them asks I wanted to grant (Ada's orchard waited from day 3 to day 7). That turned into "kept me waiting" grievances I could do nothing about.

### 3. Thinking sims: 7/10

You can find out why people do things, and the reasons are good:
- **Reasons are visible.** About pages give needs, "On their mind" with causes, the hope and its next step, and memories. The inspector traces Ada's +0.45 for the steward to "granted the town's wish: more green about the place (+0.62)" and "planted my sister's orchard (+0.91)".
- **Plans move.** Ada's three dreams in a month each had real next steps ("My next step is to wait for the orchard to be planted.", "work up the courage to talk to Juniper"), and the last one ended in an apology I could read in the log.
- **Consequences trace back to causes.** Fen's sleep complaints trace to the bakery I placed and to the light-sleeper fact. Marlow's refusal traces to the favour count on the talk panel ("Favours asked this week: 5 (that is a lot)").
- **Grief works when the attachment is real.** In the second town, Wren and Fen saw the oak gone ("Wren sees the old oak is gone: 'Kind of, we used to sit by the old oak every evening.'"). Two days later Wren said "we lost the old oak, two days ago", and their page said "It still stings that we lost the old oak". Headless, the same removal drew reactions on four of five seeds.
- **In the engaged town the oak drew nothing at all.** No line in the log or in 87 later answers. Nobody there had come to hold it dear, so that is consistent, but it does mean the choice had no visible effect.

It loses points where the mind contradicts itself or sticks:
- **Juniper dislikes her own dream.** Her page on day 30 says "The glasshouse spoils the view." Her memories on the same page say "Last summer, you built my glasshouse. I keep it like a pressed flower." The eyesore rule (`src/sim/mind/structured.ts` around lines 298 to 311) fires on any work place for someone who loves nature, without checking that the building is theirs or their dream's.
- **Fen's page contradicts itself.** In the neglected town it said "Hungry (food is running low)" under On their mind and "Needs · all met" a few lines below.
- **Ada's most important thought can't be answered.** In the oak town she said "I am seriously considering leaving the valley. I have not forgotten that the whole valley turned out for Midsummer Lanterns…", and the chips offered were "That's fair, about Midsummer Lanterns." and "I don't see Midsummer Lanterns that way."
- **Newcomers get stuck on their first step.** Otto mentioned "get to know the neighbours" in 10 of 18 answers over 12 days. In the headless `quiet` seed 9 run, the phrase appears 33 times across seven newcomers.
- **Lines with no referent remain.** Marlow said "Look at that. I made that." on day 19 with nothing made, and Wren said "I'm trying not to pester you about it." with no "it".

### 4. Opinionated sims: 7/10

Residents now disagree with each other:
- **Neighbour opinions are often cool.** Seven of the 18 I heard were cool or worse: "Don't sit me next to Wren!" (Bram), "Rather keep clear of Marlow." (Fen), "Fen and I clash, like the wrong notes." (Juniper), "I would not say I like Bram. You see, he means well." (Ada).
- **Opinions move.** Bram went from "Don't sit me next to Wren!" on day 3 to "We're not pals" on day 14 to "Wren's all right, you know!" on day 29.
- **Places can be disliked now.** "The commons isn't her sort of place.", "The teahouse isn't her sort of place.", and Ada on the bench: "Honestly, I am not fond of the bench."
- **Decisions split the town the same way every time,** and the split matches people's values.
- **Neglect spreads standing.** On day 21 of the neglected town it ran from Ada −0.11 and Fen −0.10 through Marlow −0.28 and Juniper −0.55 down to Bram and Wren at −1.00. Juniper said "You are a storm that never breaks. Somehow, you keep me waiting."

It is not an 8 because, under an attentive steward, everyone ends up adoring you:
- **No one in the engaged town ever thought ill of me.** Of 41 "What do you think of me?" answers, none was negative and two were neutral (a newcomer's "Jury's out on you!"). Ada said "I could not ask for a better steward." on day 9 and again on day 29.
- **The grievances are real but light,** a clause on the end of praise: "Though you said no to Bram's night baking, and I am still a bit sore."
- **Talking raises standing by itself,** whatever you say. On the day-30 You tab, "heard me out" (9), "saw how I really was" (6) and "cheered me on" (3) sit beside the buildings as ▲ reasons.
- **Tastes still converge.** Four of six founders "Love growing things" and three "Can't abide a busy lane". "Nothing lifts me like growing things." closed 5 of my 180 answers.
- **Dreams converge on a new title.** Juniper, Wren and Marlow all had "Share the commons with a friend".

### 5. Expressive and interactive: 7/10

This is the biggest step up, and the writing is often a pleasure:
- **The chips now answer what was said.** "What are you hoping for?" offers two replies ("I hope it comes true." / "Is that really what you want?"), or "Take your time." / "Don't settle for too little." between dreams. Sorry and explain name the grievance actually spoken ("I'm sorry I said no to Bram's night baking.", "I'm sorry I kept you waiting 4 days for a granary.").
- **Responses are varied and in character.** 119 of 160 were distinct and none came more than four times. Doubting a dream draws "The wish goes quiet when it is questioned." from dreamy Juniper, "You are right to ask. I have asked myself the same." from formal Ada, and "Excuse me! It's my dream!" from a chatty newcomer.
- **Explaining lands.** Ada: "Thank you. I understand your reasoning now." and "That is reasonable. I withdraw my complaint, mostly." Juniper: "Ah. The shape of it changes."
- **Arguing about a neighbour lands.** "I don't see Wren that way." → "Well, I'm right, but go on!"
- **A second sorry is answered as a second sorry.** Ada: "Once was sufficient." and "I would rather see it than hear it again."
- **The answers have voice.** "Is it a crime to be this happy? Arrest me!", "Empty shelves have their own silence.", "You are a storm that never breaks."

It stays at 7 because the talk-back too often lands the same way whatever was said:
- **Pushing back on "How are you?" always wins.** I said "You don't seem it." 16 times, and the resident conceded all 16, including Bram at +1.00 standing who had just said "On top of the world, me!": "Ha! Can't fool you!". Marlow had said "Happier than I've been in ages" and answered "I'm all right. Mostly." Each concession also raises standing ("saw how I really was"). It is a single branch in `src/sim/replies.ts` (around lines 217 to 221).
- **"Don't settle for too little." gets answers written for a different question.** Marlow replied "Oh. Yes, it is. I thought you'd be pleased for me.", and so did Wren. The between-dreams pushback reuses the stances for "Is that really what you want?".
- **Sorry is forgiven at any standing.** Ada, who had just said "You have lost my good opinion entirely", replied "I must say, then let us put it behind us." Marlow went from "you don't listen" to "Come here. We are fine, you and I." The code always returns `forgiven` unless the same apology was repeated recently (`src/sim/replies.ts` around lines 236 to 245).
- **Verbal tics are glued onto canned responses.** 26 of 160 responses opened with a speaker's verbal tic: "I must say, allow me my opinion, steward.", "Kind of, I'd love to.", "Hmm, it is true whether you hold it or not.", "Like, I'm glad you think so."
- **Praise for the steward repeats word for word.** Fen said "You keep your word. That counts. Mm. You make wishes come true." on days 5 and 26, and "You do right by us. I mean that. You make wishes come true." on days 13 and 24.
- **Answers are still long.** 60 of 180 had four or five sentences, many ending on a stock line ("Not much bothers me, truly." 6 times, "I have no odd habits to speak of." 3 times).

## Verdict

**The game does not meet the bar.** No count reached 8: Fun, Thinking, Opinionated and Expressive are at 7, and Polished is at 6. Round 4 fixed what review 4 ranked first: the chips now answer what was said and hope gets a real reply. Neighbours and places have opinions with an edge. Neglect now drives people away, and gift dreams no longer flood the town. What holds the game under now is this:
- For an attentive steward the town converges on adoration, and pushing back, apologising and simply talking all raise standing.
- Several replies land the same way whatever was said.
- The first visit to the log on a phone is blank.
- A layer of raw text and layout faults remains on desktop.

## Problems, ranked by how much they hold the game back

### 1. Standing converges to the top and everyday play stops mattering

What I saw:
- All six founders "think the world of you" on day 30 (0.85 to 1.00). Not one of 41 opinions of me was negative.
- Ada called me the best possible steward on day 9.
- Mood stayed at 0.72 to 0.82 for 30 nights through three declined proposals and a lapsed one, three jobless residents and a week under a day's meals.
- Talking is rewarded in itself: "heard me out", "saw how I really was" and "cheered me on" are 18 of the ▲ reasons on the You tab.

Where:
- `src/sim/replies.ts`: agree gives +affinity with `heard_me_out` (around lines 195 to 202); disagree on a mood answer gives `saw how I really was` (around lines 217 to 221); sorry gives +0.06 affinity and forgiveness at any standing (around lines 236 to 245).

Fix:
- Make plain conversation neutral and let deeds move standing.
- Cap how much talk can add per week.
- Let low standing make an apology land as "Words are cheap" until something changes.
- Decay standing toward a resident's own baseline, so a town has to be kept, not won.
- Have unanswered work asks and lapsed proposals pull mood a little every day.

### 2. Replies that land the same way whatever was said

What I saw:
- "You don't seem it." was conceded 16 of 16 times, including by people at +1.00 who had just said they were on top of the world.
- "Don't settle for too little." was answered "Oh. Yes, it is. I thought you'd be pleased for me."
- "You have lost my good opinion entirely" was followed, after a sorry, by "then let us put it behind us".
- Ada's "I am seriously considering leaving the valley" was offered chips about Midsummer Lanterns.
- 26 responses carry a glued-on tic ("I must say, allow me my opinion, steward.").

Where: the stance choice in `src/sim/replies.ts` (around lines 203 to 245); the stance pools in `src/content/replies.ts` (`seen`, `mulled`, `bristled`); the tic in `utter`, `src/narrate/narrator.ts` around lines 350 to 361.

Fix:
- Let "You don't seem it" be denied by anyone whose mood is high ("No, really, I'm fine!"), and conceded only when mood is below their usual.
- Give the between-dreams pushback its own pool.
- Scale forgiveness by standing and by how fresh the grievance is.
- When an answer contains leaving, hunger or grief, build the chips from that sentence first.
- Don't put tics on reply responses.

### 3. The first visit to the Town log is blank, and the desktop left column hides Folk

What I saw:
- With the intro, the phone's Log tab was empty at 13:11 on day 1 (p04-nav-log) while the narrator held 23 entries.
- At 1440x900 the Folk widget vanished on days 10 and 13 and was down to a header on day 22 and in the neglected town (d10-reloaded, d13-talk-ada, d22-night, n22-talk-ada). Its cards were cut on day 30 (d30-board).

Where:
- `web/src/main.ts` lines 46 to 48 run 7.5 hours before `new Ui(...)`. `web/src/ui/ui.ts` line 430 subscribes to entries afterwards, and `rerenderLog` (around line 1310) runs only on a filter click.

Fix:
- Call `rerenderLog()` and rebuild `this.morning` from `narrator.entries` when the UI is constructed; that also fixes "Nothing new on the board." after a reload.
- Give Folk a minimum height, or collapse the Winter stores card to one line when space is short.

### 4. The board says nothing needs you when something does

What I saw: "A quiet night. Nothing needs you." on 5 of 11 mornings that had an open proposal or a work ask under it, including day 23 with three people asking for work.

Where: the fallback at the end of `renderBoard`, `web/src/ui/ui.ts` around line 1546.

Fix: when proposals or asks are open, say "Nothing new overnight" instead.

### 5. Raw lines

- "for a orchard" and "for a garden plots" (`web/src/ui/ui.ts` line 1094)

- Use `aOrAn` and the singular name in the timber message.
- Don't lowercase the bio or add a full stop after one, and skip the line for a bio the player already knows.
- Pluralise the "spoils" template.

### 6. Opinions that contradict the person who holds them

What I saw:
- Juniper: "The glasshouse spoils the view." beside "you built my glasshouse. I keep it like a pressed flower."
- Fen: "Hungry (food is running low)" over "Needs · all met".
- In the headless oak run, Bram on seed 5 went from "The old oak! Gone! I still can't believe it!" to "The old oak! Overrated!" and "Done with the old oak! Done!" within days.

Where: the eyesore and not-for-me appraisal in `src/sim/mind/structured.ts` around lines 298 to 311.

Fix:
- Exempt a resident's workplace, home and dream buildings from dislike.
- Make the needs summary agree with the On their mind list.
- Don't let a grieving resident turn on the lost place in the same week.

### 7. Convergence and stuck lines

What I saw:
- "Loves growing things" on four of six founders and "Can't abide a busy lane" on three.
- "Share the commons with a friend" as the dream of Juniper, Wren and Marlow.
- Otto on "get to know the neighbours" in 10 of 18 answers (33 times in the headless `quiet` seed 9 run).
- Identical newcomer bios across towns ("A gardener from a big estate, … wanted to live closer to growing things." for Otto and for Corin).
- Fen repeating the same praise word for word.
- Marlow's "Look at that. I made that." with nothing made.

Fix:
- Spread the love and dislike facts.
- Cap a dream title at two holders.
- Let a newcomer's first step finish after a few chats or a visit.
- Vary newcomer backstories by seed.
- Rest a whole answer, not only its lines.

### 8. Economy pacing: timber famine early, a granary that hides hunger late

What I saw:
- Seventeen builds refused for lack of timber, mostly between days 2 and 13, with timber at 1 to 5 for most of that time. Ada's orchard and Juniper's granary waited four days each, which turned into grievances.
- From day 22 to day 28 the larder held 0.8 to 12.8 food for 7 to 8 people with no worry and no hunger, because the granary fed the town from its 300.
- At spring the granary "shares out the last 196 food" and empties, and the next morning the board worries about 5 food.

Fix:
- Start with more timber, or let the first timber favour come with a hint on the board.
- Show the granary drawing down as its own worry.
- Carry part of the stores into spring instead of emptying them in a day.
