# Townlet: bar review 7 (2026-10-09)

This is the seventh independent review against the bar in `docs/GAME_DESIGN_SPEC.md` section 9.4: five counts, each scored out of 10, with 8 as the pass mark on every one. I read review 6 only for its shape, and spec 9.3 only to learn what round 6 claims to have fixed. Every number, quote and screenshot here comes from my own sessions. Screenshots are in `docs/reviews/shots-7/`: `p` is the phone, `d` the engaged desktop game and `n` the neglected desktop game.

## What I played

I built the game with `npm run build`, served it with `vite preview` on port 4173, and drove the real UI with Playwright (chromium, swiftshader). Everything a player does went through on-screen controls: the intro cards, the Build tray and a click on a tile (a tap and "✓ Place" on the phone), the Remove tool, the proposal modal, the Journal roster, and the question, reply, picker and favour chips. I placed and commanded nothing through the debug handle. I chose seeds no earlier review used: `bakery` 9, `quiet` 10 and `bakery` 10. (Bakery 7 and quiet 9 were used by reviews 4 and 5, so I stayed off them.)

- **Engaged, 30 days, desktop 1440x900, `bakery` seed 9, with the intro.** Each morning at 08:05 I read the board, then answered the day's proposal modal on a rotation: 11 modals, 6 approved, 3 declined, 2 "Decide later". One of the two came back the next day and I approved it; the other (Bram's night of baking) lapsed.
  - **Building.** I granted every ask near the asker's home (hedges and flower beds for green, the workplace named, the dream building named) and followed a light plan of my own. 22 buildings went up: a bakery, two gardens, an orchard (Ada's dream), a granary, a glasshouse (Juniper's dream), a teahouse, two banners, two cottages, two benches and seven flower beds. 5 attempts were refused: timber on days 6, 7 and 8, one bad spot, and the fountain (locked until Townlet).
  - **Talking.** Each noon I talked to three people, two questions each, rotating all five questions ("What do you think of…" alternated people and places). I replied to most answers, taking sorry or explain whenever offered, and left one in nine unanswered. That came to 198 answers and 176 replies: 76 agree, 81 disagree, 17 sorry and 2 explain.
  - **The "me" rounds.** On days 12 and 28 I asked everyone "What do you think of me?".
  - **Favours.** 34 favours, one a day from the first person I spoke to, plus four timber asks of Marlow in a row on day 9.
  - **Save and reload** on day 10: I closed the page and opened the plain URL.
  - **Removing a loved place.** On day 13 I removed the place residents' own beliefs held most dear, the commons (held dear by Marlow and Wren), and talked to them on days 14 and 15.
  - **Letting the larder run low.** On day 17 I removed every food place: both gardens, the jetty, the bakery and Ada's sister's orchard. That is also the workplace of five people and the building of Ada's dream. I granted no food asks until day 23, when I rebuilt a bakery, a garden and a jetty.
  - **Records and survey.** I recorded mood, food, timber, the granary and every resident's standing each night. On day 30 I read the You tab, all eight About pages, the Goals and Folk widgets and the log in all three filters.
  - **Outcome.** The town grew from 6 to 8 (Corin on day 12, Ida on day 26), became a Hamlet on day 5 and a Village on day 23, and ended with 424 renown.
- **Neglected, 21 days, desktop, `quiet` seed 10, with the intro.** I never built, talked or answered. The six proposals got "Decide later" because the modal blocks the screen. I read the board on days 1, 4, 7, 10, 14, 17 and 21 and the You tab on day 21. On day 22 I read all five About pages and asked Ada, Fen, Juniper, Marlow and the newcomer Hazel how, me, mind and hope, replying sorry where offered and disagree otherwise.
- **Fresh on a phone, 390x844 touch, `bakery` seed 10, with the intro.**
  - Three intro cards and Begin, then 90 real seconds at live speed, then all five nav tabs.
  - Ada from the Folk album: her page, then Talk with four questions and a reply to each.
  - A tap on Juniper in the town for the quick card, then its Favour button.
  - A bench placed by touch with the outline and "✓ Place".
  - Night from 19:00 on day 1 to 06:00 on day 2, checking the street lamps against who was awake, then a turn to landscape and back.
- **Headless.** `npm run radio` for `bakery` 12 (30 days, considerate), `quiet` 13 (30 days, no steward flag) and `quiet` 14 (30 days, favours), and `npm run inspect` for `bakery` 12.

**Crashes and console.** Nothing crashed and there were no page errors. The console showed only the three.js "PCFSoftShadowMap has been removed" warning and a failed Google font request (the sandbox proxy's certificate, not the game).

**Save and reload worked.** The game came back at the same moment (tick 13445 to 13447) with the same 31 buildings and 6 residents, the log full (214 rows) and no intro. At 390 px the page never scrolled sideways (scrollWidth 390 on every check), and the town canvas matched the stage exactly in portrait, in landscape and back again.

**Harness limits.**
- **Time.** Software rendering runs at 1 to 2 fps, so I advanced time with `runTicks` and read bubbles and toasts at moments I chose. Building and removing are applied on the next tick, so after each placement I stepped one tick.
- **Camera.** To bring a tile on screen I used the debug `focus` call, which does what the board's "Show me" button does. Placement itself was always a click on the map.
- **Timings.** The three browser sessions overlapped on four cores, so load times (2.9 to 3.6 s to the handle, 6.5 s for the reload) are slower than a real device and I do not score them.
- **Rotations, not taste.** Questions, replies, favours and proposal answers followed fixed rotations. Asking a favour of the first person every day made some people refuse for being over-asked, which a real player would notice and stop doing.
- **The famine was deliberate and harsh.** Tearing out every food place in one morning is not what a player would do; I did it to see whether the town notices.
- **One crashed run.** My first engaged run stopped on day 5 when the Hamlet card opened in the middle of a conversation and my script could not click the reply under it. I restarted from day 1; all numbers here are from the second run.

## Scores

### 1. Fun: 6/10

Some of it works:
- **Neglect bites, and you can watch it.** In the neglected town mean mood fell from 0.70 to 0.55. Bram left on day 19 and Wren on day 21; Juniper and Marlow were "thinking of leaving". The board filled with people giving up: "Ada has stopped asking you for anything. 'I will manage my own affairs from now on.'" (n21-board).
- **There is always something to decide in the first fortnight.** Asks, wishes, proposals and favours kept every morning busy, and proposals split the town by name ("Bram, Juniper would welcome it; Ada, Fen, Wren would rather not.").
- **The town surprises.** A travelling fiddler, gifts left on my step, Fen teaching Ada to fish ("Fen nods once, which from Fen is a speech."), newcomers with backstories, the spring share-out from the granary.
- **Over-asking has a cost.** Marlow on day 9: "Happy to." then "I'm still on the last thing you asked!" three times; Juniper and Ada refused later favours.

It drops below review 6's 7 because in the engaged game nothing I took away stuck:
- **Wrecking the town cost almost nothing.** I removed the commons (day 13), then five workplaces and Ada's dream orchard (day 17). Mean mood dipped from 0.82 (day 16) to between 0.71 and 0.75 for five days and was back to 0.80 by day 24. By day 30 every founder "thinks the world of you" on the You tab (d30-you), with Fen at 0.98 and Juniper and Wren at 1.00.
- **The famine never came.** By day 18 the granary held 235 food against a 180 target, so when I emptied the larder (1.2 food on day 22) the granary simply fed the town. The board said so ("The larder is nearly bare, and the granary is feeding the town: 196 food left in it.") and nobody minded. Once winter stores are full, food stops being a decision.
- **Timber goes slack.** After three timber refusals on days 6 to 8, timber piled up from 36 on day 17 to 74 on day 30, with the fountain locked behind Townlet and nothing I wanted to spend it on.
- **Proposals lean on one person.** Bram made 4 of the 10 distinct proposals, and my one unanswered proposal (his night of baking) became the whole town's grievance (see Expressive).

An hour in, the town is pleasant but it no longer pushes back; I did not want a second hour of the engaged town.

- The Folk widget keeps a whole row at 1440x900 on every desktop screenshot (d13, d14, d18, d22, n21).
- Street lamps burn while anyone is up and go out with the last sleeper. On the phone all 7 were lit at 23:30 with Wren still up, all 7 were off at 00:30 with everyone asleep, and they came back at 06:00 when Bram and Fen rose (p13-night-d1-2330, p13-night-d2-0030). The town fills the phone screen in every shot.
- "?seed=10&new=1" started seed 10. No "someone" opened a sentence, no lowercase starts, no doubled words, no "undefined".
- Touch placement worked first time, the quick card and its Favour button work, and winter snow and lit windows are lovely (d22-night).

It stays at 7 because raw text still shows in the places players read most:

### 3. Thinking sims: 6/10

You can still find out why people act:
- **About pages show the reasons.** Settled opinions have their evidence, "On their mind" names needs, plans and grievances, and "Remembers most" quotes dated memories ("I still recall that Fen taught me to fish, yesterday.").
- **Plans move.** Ada's orchard went from asking to planting to "the first apples from my orchard". Bram, Fen, Juniper and Wren all finished dreams and took new ones.
- **Loss is noticed.** The commons drew "I visit the place the commons was, and it visits me." (Juniper) and Marlow raised it unprompted for days ("It still stings that we lost the commons, yesterday.").

It drops to 6 because the minds now contradict the story they are in:
- **Marlow leaves and doesn't.** In both desktop towns: "Marlow climbs onto the trade cart with his bag. He waves until the bend in the road." (day 19). He stays. In the engaged town he went to the Harvest Supper that evening and on day 24 "Marlow has decided to stay. 'I'm staying. This is home.'" His day-30 page still says "Hoping to: decide whether to stay, or follow the trade cart. Decided to go."
- **Bram's dream completes after the fact.** I tore down his bakery on day 17; the Harvest Supper ran on day 19 without it. On day 23, the morning I rebuilt the bakery, the log said "Bram feeds the whole valley at the Harvest Supper." Four days late, with no oven on the night.
- **Losing your livelihood barely registers.** I removed Fen's jetty on day 17. He said nothing about it; his only line was "Miss the garden plots." On day 19 he was "Mm. Never better." and his standing went from 0.98 to 1.00. Bram, about his own bakery: "No more nice smell? Shame!"
- **A dream's building is not grieved at full weight.** Round 6 says it should be. The morning after I removed the orchard her late sister planned, Ada said "Quite well, thank you. I have not forgotten that we lost the orchard, yesterday." Her standing fell from 0.58 to 0.37 and was back to 0.80 by day 27. She left me a parcel of food on day 21, and on day 21 her answer to "What do you think of me?" praised me for planting the orchard.
- **Pages contradict the log.** In the neglected town Juniper's page said "Hoping to: make something for Wren. Done!" on the same morning the log said "Juniper puts away what she had in mind for Wren. Wren is gone from the valley now."
- **Opposite tastes still settle together.** Wren's day-30 opinions include both "The teahouse is where the good evenings happen." and "The teahouse isn't their sort of place." Round 6 claimed one taste per place.
- **Page and answer disagree.** On the phone at 10:03 on day 1, Ada's page said "Cross about the steward" and her answer was "So far, I have been glad of you."
- **"The whole valley turned out"** for things only two people did: "Yesterday, the whole valley turned out for a long evening at the old oak." (Juniper, after an evening with Wren).

### 4. Opinionated sims: 7/10

They have views of their own and say so:
- **Neighbours and places divide them.** "Bit of a thorn, Ida!" (Bram), "Rather keep clear of Corin." (Fen), "There is a pane of glass between Marlow and me." (Juniper), "I find Marlow rather trying." (Ada). Quarrels about the teahouse ran all month in the neglected town ("You are far too easy on the teahouse, Bram.").
- **Decisions land on both sides.** "Juniper thinks less of you: said yes to Bram's shared supper." the same morning Bram was delighted.
- **The neglected town turned on me.**
  - Ada: "You have disappointed me, if I may say so. You see, your ears are elsewhere."
  - Juniper: "You are a storm that never breaks."
  - Hazel, three days after arriving: "You? Hmm! Could do better!"
- **Tastes differ.** Six founders, six different dreams; tastes in places (brook, teahouse, old oak, flower bed) vary by person.

It is a 7, not an 8, because their regard for you can be bought back too easily:
- **No one in the engaged town thought ill of me.** 0 of 48 "What do you think of me?" answers were negative. 14 carried a grievance ("Though you took away the garden plots, and that stung a little."), but every one opened kindly or neutrally.
- **Every apology landed.** 17 of 17 sorries in the engaged town were accepted, including Marlow's for removing his favourite place ("Oh, thank you. That helps, it really does.") and Corin's for removing his workplace (a tilted head). The worst was "Took you long enough. But all right."
- **Apologies land with people who have lost faith.** In the neglected town Juniper, at −0.60 standing and "has lost faith in you", took a sorry with "I will try to let it go."; Ada at −0.22 with "Then let us put it behind us."
- **Standing hits the ceiling.** Wren went from 0.95 to 1.00 across losing her favourite place (the commons, day 13) and her workplace (a garden, day 17), and never fell below 0.90 after day 15.

### 5. Expressive and interactive: 7/10

Talking is still the best thing in the game:
- **The voices are distinct and a pleasure.** "The trees and I have an understanding." (Juniper), "Modest AND good! Unbearable!" (Bram), "Rest is not retreat. But I hear you." (Ada), "You can't talk me out of it, you know." (Corin).
- **Chips answer what was said.** Sorry and explain name the real grievance ("I'm sorry I took away the commons."); hope answers between dreams offer "Take your time." and "Don't settle for too little."
- **Responses vary.** 122 of 174 responses were distinct, and none came more than three times.
- **Explain is offered only for decisions** (proposals said yes, no or left unanswered); lapsed asks get sorry only. Round 6 holds here.

It drops from 8 to 7 because a month of talk wears thin and some exchanges don't fit:
- **Stock clauses everywhere.**
  - "Do you know, I still smile about it: …" 10 times.
  - "I like you. You listen." 7 times each.
  - "What matters to me is …" 8 times, "Nothing lifts me like …" 8, "I work at …" 7, "…, more than most." 7.
  - Refusals are word for word: "I've done a lot for you this week. I need a break." 4 times; "You ask and ask. I need some days of my own." 4 times.
- **Answers padded with facts.** Of 198 answers, 19 ran to four sentences, usually ending on a stock fact: "I am in good order. I am making progress. Next, I must tell a close friend about my sister's orchard. I work at the garden plots."
- **"You don't seem it" never lands.** I said it 18 times; every resident denied it, because in this town everyone was always well. It is the only challenge chip on "How are you?", and it never leads anywhere.
- **Chips that don't fit the answer.**
  - Corin, "I don't know you well enough yet, I think." got one chip: "Thank you. That means something."
  - "I don't see it that way." was offered against Wren's "I keep checking the board for an answer."
  - Bram, told "You don't seem it" when he was well: "Well, since you ask properly!"
- **One lapse becomes everyone's line.** On day 28, after Bram's night of baking lapsed, five people opened their "me" answer with the same grievance in the same shape: "Though you never answered Bram's night baking, and that stung a little." Ida had been in town two days. Juniper raised it again in her next answer after accepting my apology for it the day before.

## Verdict

**The game does not meet the bar.** No count reaches 8: Fun 6, Polished 7, Thinking 6, Opinionated 7, Expressive 7.

Round 6 fixed what review 6 listed:
- A rescue builds count as answers.
- Grievances are in the first person.
- The Folk widget keeps a row.
- No proposal greets a phone player before the town.
- Explain is for decisions.
- The night is right on a phone.

This review pushed harder on consequences, and that is where the game gives way. A steward who tears out the commons, five workplaces and a dream orchard ends the month adored by every founder. The granary makes food a solved problem by mid-month. The story tells you Marlow has left and Bram has fed the valley when neither happened. The voices are still a pleasure, but over a month they lean on the same clauses, and every apology works.

## Problems, ranked by how much they hold the game back

### 1. What you take away barely costs you: standing shrugs off loss and every apology lands

What I saw:
- Removing the commons (loved by Marlow and Wren), then both gardens, the jetty, the bakery and Ada's sister's orchard, cost a five-day mood dip (0.82 to 0.71) and nothing lasting.
- Day 30: all six founders "think the world of you"; Fen 0.98, Juniper 1.00, Wren 1.00. Wren rose from 0.95 to 1.00 across losing her favourite place and her job.
- Fen's standing rose the day after his jetty went. Ada was at 0.80 ten days after losing her sister's orchard and left me a gift in between.
- 17 of 17 sorries accepted in the engaged town; Juniper at −0.60 accepted one in the neglected town.

Where:
- A lost place weighs at most `LOSS_WEIGHT = 0.09` on mood and fades over `LOSS_DAYS = 4` (`src/sim/sim.ts` lines 131 to 132 and 1417).
- A sorry is refused only below −0.5 standing and for a grievance three days old or more (`src/sim/replies.ts` around line 278). Fresh grievances always soften.
- Nothing marks taking away someone's workplace as a grievance on its own; Fen's jetty drew no reaction.

Fix:
- Make taking away someone's workplace or dream building a lasting grievance against the steward ("took away my jetty"). It should cap their standing for a couple of weeks, the way the low larder caps it, not only weigh on mood for four days.
- Let a sorry be refused when the loss is large and recent ("Not yet. You took my jetty.") or when trust is low, whatever the date.
- Keep standing off its ceiling while any grievance is open, so "thinks the world of you" has to be earned back.

### 2. The economy goes slack after the first fortnight

What I saw:
- The granary passed its 180 target by day 15 and reached 235 by day 18.
- With every food place removed, the larder fell to 1.2 on day 22 and the granary fed the town with no mood cost; the board noted it as a worry and nothing else.
- Timber: three refusals on days 6 to 8, then 36 on day 17 rising to 74 on day 30 with nothing to buy (the fountain needs Townlet).
- In the neglected town timber sat at 61 on day 21.

Where: the granary feeding the town is a board worry only (`src/sim/stores.ts` and the larder terms in `src/sim/sim.ts` around line 1417). Tier gates (`src/sim/progress.ts`) lock the one big timber sink.

Fix:
- Let eating from the winter stores before winter worry people ("We are eating the winter stores in autumn"). Count a larder fed only from the granary as a short larder for mood, at half weight.
- Give mid-game timber somewhere to go: open one larger build (a fountain or a second teahouse) at Hamlet, or add upkeep, so surplus is a choice and not a pile.

### 3. The story contradicts itself

What I saw:
- Marlow "climbs onto the trade cart with his bag" on day 19 in both desktop towns and stays. On day 24 he "decided to stay", and his page still says "Decided to go."
- Bram "feeds the whole valley at the Harvest Supper" four days after it, the morning his bakery was rebuilt.
- Juniper's page said "make something for Wren. Done!" while the log said she put it away.
- Wren holds both "The teahouse is where the good evenings happen." and "The teahouse isn't their sort of place."
- "The whole valley turned out for a long evening at the old oak" for an evening two people spent.

Where:
- `marlow:decide:leave` in `src/content/story.ts` line 143 is told when the "decide" stage is entered. It describes leaving, but entering only sets `leaving` for a week (`src/sim/story/aspirations.ts` around line 333). `aspiration.outcome` stays `'leave'` after he decides to stay, so the page (`web/src/ui/ui.ts` line 2087) keeps "Decided to go."
- Bram's last stage checks for any Harvest Supper since the dream began and any bakery now (`src/sim/story/aspirations.ts` around line 171), so it fires whenever a bakery returns.
- The `wonderful_time` memory clause is always "the whole valley turned out for" (`src/narrate/narrator.ts` line 1005).

Fix:
- Tell Marlow's leaving line only when he departs. When a "leaving" turns to "staying", set the outcome to `stay`.
- Make Bram's feast check run on the supper night only (a bakery standing that evening), or let it lapse with its own line ("No ovens for the supper. Next year.").
- Mark a gift dream as given up, not done, when its recipient leaves.
- Reuse the round 6 "one taste per place" retirement for the evening and fit pair (good_times against not_for_me), which still both settle.
- Say "we spent a long evening at the old oak" when the attendees are not most of the town.

### 4. Raw lines in the most-read places

What I saw, with where each comes from:
- "Fen sees the garden plots is gone" (`src/narrate/narrator.ts` lines 786 and 787 use "is" for every name).
- "Fen asks a friend, gruffly, whether they want to learn to fish. A friend says yes." (`src/narrate/narrator.ts` line 757 falls back to "a friend" when the pupil isn't chosen yet; `fen:student` in `src/content/story.ts` line 127).
- "Bram, Fen and 3 more ask for a place to work: a bakery". The grouped card takes `wants` from the first ask only (`web/src/ui/ui.ts` around line 1534).
- "Lives in: Bram and Hazel's cottage" after Bram left. `subjectName` lists departed owners (`src/narrate/narrator.ts` line 281).
- "I must say, I would say", "Just... Show me."
- The Hamlet card opening over waiting reply chips.
- Six "You read X's story on his page." log lines.
- The winter stores card cut to its title at 1440x900.

Fix:
- Use "are" for plural place names.
- Name the pupil, or tell this beat only once a pupil exists.
- Group workplace asks by the building wanted.
- Leave departed residents out of a home's name.
- Allow one tic per sentence.
- Hold tier cards until the talk panel is idle.
- Keep page reads out of the "Everything" log or fold them into one line.
- Give the stores card a one-line summary when Goals is short.

### 5. A month of talk leans on stock clauses, and some chips don't fit

What I saw:
- "Do you know, I still smile about it" 10 times; "I like you. You listen." 7; "What matters to me is" 8; "Nothing lifts me like" 8.
- 19 four-sentence answers ending on a fact ("I work at the garden plots.").
- "You don't seem it" denied 18 of 18 times.
- A single "Thank you. That means something." chip under "I don't know you well enough yet".
- One lapsed proposal voiced by five people in one template on one day, and again after it was forgiven.

Where:
- Trait facts and "what matters to me" are appended to answers to reveal Folk facts (`src/narrate/narrator.ts`, `ANSWER_SENTENCES`).
- The memory clause openers ("Do you know, I still smile about it:") come from a small pool.
- The "me" chips are fixed for a neutral answer.
- A grievance about someone else's proposal is shared by everyone who wanted it.

Fix:
- Reveal a fact once and then stop appending it.
- Widen the memory openers per voice and rest each one for a week.
- On "How are you?", offer a different challenge to someone who is well ("What's the best thing this week?").
- Give a neutral "me" answer neutral chips ("Fair. Let's get to know each other.").
- Let only the proposer and one or two others voice a lapsed proposal, and let a forgiven grievance drop out of answers for that resident.

### 6. Later dream steps stall for a week

What I saw: Fen's "teach someone at the jetty" was his line on days 4 to 11 in the neglected town and days 4 to 10 in the engaged one. Marlow's "make up my mind before the cart's last visit" ran nine days. Bram's "Bake for the whole valley at the Harvest Supper! Next up: bake for everyone at the Harvest Supper!" says the same thing twice.

Where: round 6 bounded only first steps. Middle steps wait on calendar checks (`dayOf >= 23` for Marlow) or on chance meetings.

Fix: give every step a days-based fallback, as the first step has. Write the "next" line so it differs from the dream title.
