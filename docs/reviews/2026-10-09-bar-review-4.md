# Townlet: bar review 4 (2026-10-09)

Fourth independent review against the bar in `docs/GAME_DESIGN_SPEC.md` section 9.4: five counts, each out of 10, pass mark 8 on every one. I read the three earlier reviews only to know what had been claimed fixed and to match their shape; every number, quote and screenshot here comes from my own sessions. Screenshots are in `docs/reviews/shots-4/` (`p` phone, `d` engaged desktop, `n` neglected desktop, `f` the famine session).

## What I played

I built the game with `npm run build`, served it with `vite preview` on 4173 and drove the real UI with Playwright (chromium, swiftshader). Every build, answer, question, reply, favour and removal below went through the on-screen controls: the Build tray and a click on a tile, the Remove tool, the proposal modal, the Journal roster, and the question, reply and favour chips. Nothing was placed by command except in the famine session, noted below.

- **Engaged, 30 days, desktop 1440x900, `bakery` seed 2.** At 08:05 each morning I read the board, answered each proposal in the modal on a fixed rotation (10 modals: approved Marlow's cart stop, Wren's lantern walk, Bram's tales night on its second posting, Marlow's market and Bram's night baking; declined Juniper's contraption twice and Bram's bonfire; "Decide later" on Bram's tales night on day 9 and his second bonfire on day 28, which then lapsed). I placed 16 buildings through the tray: flower bed and bakery on day 1, then a hedge, four cottages, garden plots, two benches, an orchard, a granary, a second flower bed, the town banner and a second hedge. A fountain on day 27 was refused because it is still locked. At noon I talked to three residents through the Journal, two questions each, rotating all five questions (how, mind, hope, me, "what do you think of…") and replying after every answer on a rotation of agree, disagree, sorry and explain. That came to 180 answers and 180 replies: 101 agree, 60 disagree, 15 sorry, 4 explain. I asked 33 favours, including four timber requests to Marlow in one morning on day 16. On day 10 I saved and reopened the plain URL. On day 12 I took the old oak down with the Remove tool, and on day 13 I asked everyone "What do you think of me?" and said sorry wherever I was offered the chance. I built no food after the orchard on day 11, and the larder sat at 0 to 6 food from day 22 to day 28. I recorded mood, food and every resident's standing nightly, and on day 30 read the board, the You tab, all eleven About pages, the Goals and Folk widgets and the log in all three filters. The town grew from 6 to 11 people (one newcomer, Rufus, left on day 24). It became a Hamlet on day 7 and a Village on day 27, with 347 renown.
- **Neglected, 21 days, desktop, `quiet` seed 3.** I never built, talked or answered. I pressed "Decide later" on the six proposal modals because they block the screen, read the board on days 1, 7, 14 and 21 and the You tab on day 21. On day 22 I read three About pages and asked Ada, Fen and Juniper how, me and mind, taking sorry or disagree where offered.
- **Famine, desktop, `bakery` seed 2, days 1 to 21.** I wanted a real stretch of hunger, so in a separate town I answered every proposal yes, placed a bakery and a flower bed by command on day 1, then on day 12 took down the garden plots, the jetty and the bakery with the Remove tool and built nothing more. I read the board and talked to four residents (how, mind, me, sorry where offered) on days 13, 16 and 19.
- **Fresh on a phone, 390x844 touch, `bakery` seed 7, with the intro**, at live speed. I went through the three intro cards and let 90 real seconds of the town run, answering the proposal that popped up. Then I tapped all five nav tabs, opened Ada from the Folk album (About, then Talk with four questions and replies), tapped Bram on the map for the quick card, and placed a flower bed with the touch outline and "✓ Place".
- **Headless.** I ran `npm run radio` for `bakery` seed 5 (30 days, considerate), `quiet` seed 6 (30 days, none) and `quiet` seed 8 (30 days, favours), and `npm run inspect` for `bakery` seed 5 at day 14. I used these for repeated lines and to check reasons against what the pages say.

**Crashes and console.** Nothing crashed and there were no page errors. The console had the three.js warning "PCFSoftShadowMap has been removed. Using PCFShadowMap instead." and one failed request (ERR_CERT_AUTHORITY_INVALID), which is the sandbox proxy blocking the web font, not the game. The page falls back to a system font, so the typeface I saw is not the intended one. Save and reload came back at the same tick (14220) with the same 50 buildings and 8 residents in 7.5 s, with no intro. The first frame took 2.6 to 3.2 s on desktop and 3.6 s on the phone. At 390 px the page never scrolled sideways on any screen I opened (scrollWidth 390 on every check).

**Harness limits.** Software rendering runs at 1 to 2 fps, so I advanced time with `runTicks` between actions rather than watching it play. That means I read bubbles and toasts at moments of my choosing, not as a player would meet them. Questions, replies and building spots followed a fixed rotation, not taste. My script only answered "asks for more green" cards that named one person, so the grouped green cards ("Fen, Ada and Wren ask for more green") mostly went unanswered. The town's green wish lapsing is partly my play, not the game. On the phone, the first tap on the intro's Next button timed out in Playwright but still landed a moment later; every later tap worked, and the touch placement built the flower bed. The famine session was a separate town with all its food buildings removed on purpose, because the engaged town never actually went hungry.

## Scores

### 1. Fun: 7/10

There is a decision most mornings, and the decisions now cost something. Ten proposals came in 30 days, each listing who is for and against, and the sides match the people. Ada and Fen are against every noisy thing; Bram, Marlow, Wren and the newcomers are for. Letting one lapse lands: "Bram's bonfire on the commons went unanswered. Bram takes it badly. Ada, Fen and Lotte are glad. Juniper, Marlow, Wren, Gideon, Rosa, Ash and Wilf are not pleased." Favours can now be refused on my account. Ten of 33 were refused, five of them about me: "Why would I? You don't do much for us." (Fen, days 10 and 29, and Rufus, day 14), "Hmm, I don't know you well enough to do that." (Juniper), "I'm sorry, I don't feel I can, the way things are." (Wren and Rosa). Residents give up on me in their own words: "Wren has stopped asking you for anything. 'I've stopped asking. It hurt less than waiting.'" and "Ada has stopped asking you for anything. 'I have stopped expecting anything, which is a kind of peace.'" Neglect has teeth now: Bram left the neglected town on day 19 and Wren went to −1.00 standing. A real famine also bites. After I took out the food buildings, mean mood went 0.74 → 0.68 → 0.54 between days 12 and 19 and two people left by day 22. The tiers, the winter-stores worry and newcomers walking in with their own dreams ("I'd love to put a roof over people who need one") give the month a shape. The oak got six named reactions within hours ("The valley is poorer without the old oak."). That is a game I would play a second hour of.

It stops at 7 because, in ordinary play, nothing I did changed how the town felt. Mean mood in the engaged game stayed between 0.74 and 0.79 on all 30 nights. That held through the oak, two declined proposals, a week with the larder at 0 to 6 food for 11 people, and Rufus leaving. Over 21 days of total neglect, mood went from 0.75 to 0.70. Every "What are you hoping for?" offers only "That's fair.", and residents between dreams say "I did it, you know! I'm still thinking what comes next." (8 times). By day 30, six of eleven dreams were "make something for…" or "do something for the steward". The board on day 26 said "A quiet night. Nothing needs you." with 6 food in the larder for 11 people.

### 2. Polished: 6/10

The code-form text from review 3 is gone. I searched all my sessions' text for "matters (", bracketed ids, "you our", "you nothing", "Maybe The" and "You does/has/is" and found none. The tier modal and the log both say "send 8 timber". Same-kind asks are one card ("Bram, Fen and 9 more ask for more food") with one hint. The talk panel keeps the last three exchanges (d13-talk-ada, f19-talk, p07-ada-talk). The phone layout holds on every screen (p01 to p09), the quick card is clean (p08), the proposal modal reads well on a phone (p03), and touch placement worked (p09). Night, rain and snow scenes are lovely (d22-night, n22-talk-ada).

It is held to 6 by a list of things that look wrong to a player:

- **Reply chips go stale.** If you don't reply, the next answer keeps the previous answer's chips whenever both offer the same kinds. Juniper, in famine week: "You make the valley feel looked after…" offered "Good. I'm glad." / "You don't seem it.", which were the "How are you?" chips. Bram's "Nothing much, honestly. The panel keys its chips on the reply kinds only (`p.replies.dataset.key = open.map((o) => o.kind).join('|')`, `web/src/ui/ui.ts` around line 1781), so the text is never redrawn.

- **Dream cards quote the wrong dream.** "Juniper asks for help with a dream: a granary. 'Share the commons with a friend.'" (famine day 19) and "…a granary. 'Make something for Bram.'" The inspector traces Ada's +0.85 for the steward to "green by my door (+0.68)", "planted my sister's orchard (+0.91)" and three "told Fen/Wren" reinforcements. Plans move now. Marlow, who spent review 3 not deciding about the cart, said "make up my mind before the cart's last visit" on days 3, 8 and 9 and "I did it, you know!" on day 12. Ada's orchard dream finished and she said so ("Honestly, I have done what I hoped to. Something new will come."). Hunger works through needs into speech when it is real. ### 5. Expressive and interactive: 6/10

The writing is often a pleasure, and the talk-back now has range. 137 of my 180 reply responses were distinct, and none came up more than four times. Responses fit the person and the moment. It stays at 6 because a player keeps hitting the seams:

- **Answers still carry bolted-on facts.** "I can't abide noise, especially at night." (9), "I need a good long sleep, more than most." (8), "My favourite spot is the commons." ## Verdict

**Does not meet the bar.** No count reached 8; four are at 6 or 7 and Polished is at 6. This round fixed most of what review 3 ranked: the raw text, the stock reply pool, the ten identical cards, refusals that never mention you, a famine nobody feels, and neglect without consequences. The game is noticeably better. What holds it under now is subtler. Ordinary play never moves anyone's mood. The conversation's chips and stage directions often don't match what was just said. Dreams and pages hold on to people who have left. Opinions of people and places are nearly all warm. And every founder shares the same two habits.

## Problems, ranked by how much they hold the game back

### 1. Reply chips and responses that don't match what was said

What I saw: chips left over from the previous answer whenever I didn't reply (Juniper's "You make the valley feel looked after…" offered "Good. I'm glad." / "You don't seem it."). Chips naming a subject the answer never mentioned ("That's fair, about Blossom Day." under Ada's answer about sleeping lightly). Sorry chips naming an old grievance instead of the one just spoken (Fen's larder complaint offered sorry for the cart stop; Rufus's oak complaint offered sorry for the green wish). Praise chips under a complaint (Fen, day 20). Stage directions that ignore the tone: "(Ada gives you a warm look.)" after "you have lost my good opinion entirely" (f19-talk), "(Fen looks pleased.)" after "You don't listen. Never have." "What are you hoping for?" offered one chip in 35 of 35 answers.

Where: the chip key in `refreshTalkPanel`, `web/src/ui/ui.ts` around line 1781, compares kinds only. The offers come from `r.lastAnswer.offers` (sim `talk.ts` / `replies.ts`), which keeps a subject after the answer-trimming has dropped the sentence that named it.

Fix: key the chips on kind plus text, or on the answer's tick. Build offers from the sentences that survive trimming. Make sorry and explain address the grievance the answer actually states. Pick stage directions by valence and standing. Give hope answers a disagree ("Is that really what you want?") and an encouragement chip.

### 2. Everyday play never moves anyone's mood

What I saw: engaged mean mood 0.74 to 0.79 on all 30 nights (minimum 0.61 to 0.73) through the oak's removal, the larder at 0.9, 0.1, 0.7, 4.3, 6.2, 1.3 and 0 food on days 22 to 28 for 11 people, and a departure. Neglect: 0.75 to 0.70 over 21 days while Bram left and Wren reached −1.00. "Quite well, thank you" from neglected Ada; "A quiet night. Nothing needs you." with 6 food for 11. Only removing every food building moved mood (0.74 to 0.54 in seven days).

Fix: let standing with the steward, lapsed asks and a near-empty larder feed mood a little every day, not only an empty one. Let the "How are you?" band follow. Flag a larder below a day's meals on the board as a worry.

### 3. Dreams and pages keep people who have left, and one dream finishes itself

What I saw: "Bram has ovens of his own at last." on day 9 of a neglected town with no bakery. Ada's "make something for Rufus… give it to Rufus" after he left, and Juniper's "make something for Bram" after he left. Rufus kept in the green-progress line and the "season ended" note days after leaving. "Make something for Rufus" listed as a known fact on Ada's page. Dream ask cards quoting a different dream from the one that asked ("a granary. 'Share the commons with a friend.'").

Where: the `ovens` stage in `src/sim/story/aspirations.ts` (around lines 135 to 141) needs `until: 'bakery'` or must be excluded from the nine-day rule (around lines 459 to 463). The `gift` template in `src/sim/story/dreams.ts` (around lines 194 to 217) should let go when the recipient departs. The green-progress line and season-end note should filter departed residents. The dream card (`web/src/ui/ui.ts` around lines 1486 to 1488) should quote the dream that posted the request, or close the request when that dream ends.

### 4. Opinions of people and places are nearly all warm, and tastes converge

What I saw: 0 of 15 opinions of a neighbour were negative (one neutral), and 10 of the 15 ended with "X is my closest friend here". Settled place views were "a lovely spot" 26 times and "where the good evenings happen" 14 times, with no dislikes. All six founders "Need plenty of rest" and "Can't abide noise, especially at night", Bram included. Bram at +1.00 and Marlow at +0.98 through the empty-larder week. Ada said "I could not ask for a better steward." on day 4.

Fix: let values produce dislikes (Ada and Fen on the bakery's noise and the market, Bram on the quiet places). Add negative place shapes ("too loud at night", "an eyesore", "where Bram holds court"). Let a cool relationship show in "What do you think of X". Spread rest and noise traits so they distinguish people. Keep a hungry week from leaving anyone at the top of the standing scale.

### 5. Answers still carry stock facts and lose coherence

What I saw: "I can't abide noise, especially at night." in 9 answers, "I need a good long sleep, more than most." in 8, "My favourite spot is the commons." in 5, and "X is my closest friend here" on most person opinions. Fifty-seven of 180 answers had four or five sentences. "Look at that. I made that." in Marlow's grief for the oak. "The flower bed is all right. The flower bed is a lovely spot. I haven't found a favourite spot yet." "You listen. Well, you listen." "I did it, you know! I'm still thinking what comes next." eight times between dreams.

Fix: add a known fact only when the question is about it or the fact is new to the player. Never repeat the subject's verdict within one answer. Give the between-dreams hope a few lines that say what they are enjoying meanwhile.

### 6. Fact and page text that reads raw

What I saw: "any noise at night wakes them" (lowercase, wrong pronoun) beside "Sleeps lightly", "Needs plenty of rest" and "Can't abide noise" on Ada's page (d30-about-ada). "▲ Day 30: the steward sent Ash round" on the You tab. "(Approve or decline on the board.)" under a proposal that is a modal. "We're running out of food. Could we grow more?" four or five times in one morning's THIS MORNING. Lotte thanking me for garden plots I did not build that day.

Where: `src/sim/progress.ts` line 96 (the `light_sleeper` text is split on ";").

Fix: capitalise and pronoun-resolve both halves, or keep one line; merge facts that say the same thing; use "you" in standing notes; rotate the hunger ask lines per speaker; check what the "built for me" memory is attached to.

### 7. Dreams converge on gifts

What I saw: on day 30, Ada was hoping to "make something for Rufus", Juniper "make something for Bram", Wren "make something for Juniper", and Bram, Marlow and Gideon all "do something for the steward". That is six of eleven. Fen's "Teach someone to fish properly" was the same answer six times.

Fix: count "do something for the steward" against the gift cap. Give each trade a second dream. Let a hope answer say how it is going rather than restate the title.

### 8. Layout faults

What I saw: the Folk widget showing two cards cut off at the bottom on every 1440x900 screenshot (d30-board, d30-log-story). The worn brown slab covering the middle of a town nobody built in (n21-board, n22-talk-ada).

Fix: give Folk the remaining column height, or collapse Goals when Folk is opened. Fade wear back to grass on tiles not walked for a few days.
