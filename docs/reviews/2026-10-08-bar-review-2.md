# Townlet: bar review 2 (2026-10-08)

Second independent review against the bar in `docs/GAME_DESIGN_SPEC.md` section 9.4. Five counts, each out of 10, pass mark 8. Screenshots are in `docs/reviews/shots-2/` (prefix `p` phone, `d` engaged desktop, `n` neglected desktop). I read bar review 1 only to know what was claimed fixed; every number and quote below is from my own sessions.

## What I played

Build `npm run build`, served by `vite preview` on 4173, driven through the real UI with Playwright (chromium, swiftshader). In the browser a scenario's scripted steward commands are stripped, so every build, answer and word was mine.

- **Engaged, 30 days, desktop 1440x900, `bakery` seed 2.** Each morning at 08:05 I read the board and counted asks, wishes and proposals; followed a build plan (two flower beds, a hedge and a bakery on day 1, cottages on days 3, 11, 18 and 24, garden day 5, bench day 7, woodlot day 9, orchard day 14, granary day 16, beehives day 20, glasshouse day 22, chicken coop day 26, town banner day 28); answered proposals in the modal (approved Marlow's market on day 1, declined Bram's night baking on day 18, left Juniper's contraption on "Decide later" both times it was posted); talked to three people a day through the Journal tab, rotating all five questions including "What do you think of…" with a random subject, and after every answer clicked one of the offered replies and read what came back: 102 questions, 102 replies (52 of them "I'm sorry", because I always took it when it was offered, to see what it did), 17 favours (timber, catch, garden, clear, visit, mend, and four asks of Marlow in one morning to make him refuse). On day 10 I saved and reopened the plain URL. On day 12 I took down the old oak with the Remove tool, and on day 13 asked all ten residents "What do you think of me?" and said sorry to each of the ten who offered it. Nightly I recorded mean and minimum mood and every resident's standing with me. I read the You tab on days 20 and 30, the log in all three filters, the Goals pane, the Folk album and every About page on day 30. The town grew from 6 to 10 and reached Village on day 20.
- **Neglected, 21 days, desktop, `quiet` seed 4.** Never built, never talked, never answered; pressed "Decide later" on every modal because it blocks the screen. Read the board on days 1, 7, 14 and 21, the You tab on day 22, and asked Ada how, mind and me on day 22.
- **Fresh on a phone, 390x844 touch, `bakery` seed 2, with the intro,** at live speed for the first real-time minutes: the three intro cards, the five nav tabs, the build tray, the Folk album, Ada's About and Talk pages with four questions, and the quick card from tapping Bram in the town.
- **Headless:** `npm run radio` for `bakery` seed 3 (21 days, `considerate` and `none`) and `quiet` seed 7 (30 days, `favours`); `npm run inspect` for `bakery` seed 3 at day 14. Used for asks per day, proposal counts, repeated lines, and the evidence chains behind opinions.

Nothing crashed. The console had only the three.js shadow-map warning and one blocked Google Fonts request (the sandbox's proxy, not the game, but the page does lean on an outside font host).

## Scores

### 1. Fun: 5/10

The first ten minutes work: four asks, two wishes and a proposal on day 1, and a build that is answered at once ("Bram spots the new bakery: 'Now THAT is progress!'"). The engaged board never went empty (asks per day `4,0,1,2,3,4,4,4,4,4,5,6,6,6,6,5,5,5,6,5,4,4,3,3,3,3,3,3,11,12`), but from day 6 to day 28 the cards were the same parked dream asks (Ada's orchard, Juniper's glasshouse and granary, Wren's banner) until I built them, not things happening; in the `considerate` radio run thirteen of the last fourteen mornings had zero asks. There are still three proposal types (`DilemmaType` in `src/sim/types.ts`), four proposals in 30 days, and the contraption modal reappeared sixteen times after "Decide later", three and four days after posting, though the modal says proposals "lapse after two days". Choices do now have edges: leaving the contraption took Juniper from +0.52 to −0.48, and the You tab credits opponents for a lapse. But the biggest thing that happened in my town made no difference: the larder sat at zero on days 24, 25 and 28 (food 0.4 to 1.0 with ten mouths) and mean mood stayed 0.77 to 0.79 through it, one line ("Juniper makes do with a meagre meal") and a wall of identical asks on day 29. Hamlet came on day 2, timber was never short after the Hamlet gift (35 on day 2, 59 unspent on day 30). The moments that earn a second hour are real but rare: "Marlow lets the trade cart go without him. 'Turns out I live here.'"; "Wren lands a fish alone. Fen nods once, which from Fen is a speech."; "Wren's banner goes up. Everyone finds their own house in it."

### 2. Polished: 5/10

The shell is handsome, the night scene with lit windows (d30-you) is lovely, the phone layout holds (no sideways scroll on any tab, 44px controls, 3.9 s to first frame), the save came back identical after a 7.7 s reload with no intro, and widgets drag. What breaks it is visible in most sessions. On the phone a proposal modal covered the town at 08:49 on day 1, twenty real seconds after "Begin", before I had tapped anyone (p03b). The town centre is a brown trampled slab by day 5 with nothing built (d05), the whole middle by day 12 (d12), and a mud rectangle inside the snow in winter (n21, n22). The Goals widget clips its own tier card at a fixed height ("Hamlet" cut in half, d05; "65 ✦ to Village: it opens a chicken" cut mid-sentence, d12). After I replied "I don't see it that way", Wren's bubble over the town read **"Wren: I don't see it that way."**, my words under her name (d05; the `reply` case in `src/narrate/narrator.ts` puts the steward's line in the first quotes). Bubbles and the quick card truncate mid-sentence ("Now: socialize at the old oak until …", "came here to grow old…", p07), and "socialize" is the one American spelling in a British-voiced game. The neglected board on day 21 reads "**0 of 0 who wished for it have it: Bram.**" after Bram left. The You tab shows up arrows on grievances ("▲ Day 20: kept me waiting; let Marlow's market day quietly drop", n22-you; "▲ Day 29: let the larder run bare; said sorry", d30-you). Juniper's About page lists her bio twice (header and fact 7); Abel's shows "Happiest at home; Sleeps lightly; any noise at night wakes them; Up before everyone else" as one semicolon-joined fact and "joy about the brook, annoyance about the brook" in one feelings line. The log prints bare reasons ("Kit thinks less of the steward: built the workshop.") and stacked tics ("Like, steward! The bakery at four in the morning, you know? Any chance of a fix?").

### 3. Thinking sims: 7/10

Still the strongest count. The About page gives needs, "On their mind" with causes ("Wants some quiet (too much company for their liking)", "You: doesn't trust you (how the steward has let them down)"), the hope and the next step, what they are doing and until when, "Remembers most" with dates, and opinions with the ones still forming ("Maybe the brook is a lovely spot…"); the inspector traces Ada's +0.45 for the steward to "granted the town's wish: proper places to work (+0.27)", "planted my sister's orchard (+0.91)". Dreams chain: Juniper's glasshouse went up on day 22 and her page moved on to "make something for Wren". Memories age in speech ("yesterday", "three days ago", "last week"). It loses points because plans stall on the steward and nobody moves without you (Ada's "My next step is to wait for the orchard to be planted" from day 5 to day 14, Marlow's "First I have to watch the trade cart come and go" on days 2, 7 and 9, Juniper asking for the glasshouse on days 14 and 21 of the neglected town), and because page and mouth disagree: on neglected day 22 Ada's card says "Ada is unhappy with you" while her "How are you?" is "Quite well, thank you… I find I am in rather good spirits" and her "What do you think of me?" is "Frankly, you have failed us." On day 1 of the engaged game she said "I am grateful to you. I should have said so sooner… Frankly, you still irritate me" in one breath, and Abel, "A fisher from the estuary", "wanted to live closer to growing things".

### 4. Opinionated sims: 6/10

They disagree with each other and with me, visibly: proposals list sides, the You tab shows Ada pleased that I let Juniper's contraption drop and declined Bram's baking, Juniper and Ada have words ("You never listen, Ada. The teahouse is good, and you know it"), Wren rounds on Ada over me ("I can't believe you're on the steward's side, Ada"), and after 21 days of neglect the standings ran from Marlow +0.11 to Wren −1.0 with Bram gone on day 20. One favour was refused for a reason about me (Dunstan at −0.45: "I'm sorry, I don't feel I can, the way things are"), which is the right idea. But approval is still cheap and forgiving: two flower beds and a bakery got Ada to "I could not ask for a better steward" on day 3 and Fen to "Steward listens. Rare thing."; on day 30, after a week with the larder at zero, sixteen ignored contraption modals and the oak felled, six of ten residents "think the world of you" and three of those credit "The steward makes wishes come true"; Juniper went from −0.48 on day 17 back to +0.54 by day 30 without me ever answering her proposal. Opinions of places are mostly empty: of nine "What do you think of…" answers about buildings, six were "No view on the well", "The well is a blank page to me", "I haven't felt anything about the bench yet", "I don't really know the workshop well enough". Sixteen of seventeen favours were a yes or "I'm still on the last thing you asked!", and Marlow at +0.16 said "Happy to." Newcomers arrive with the stock dream "Settle into the valley" and already fond of you: Bertie came into the neglected town on the day Bram walked out and "thinks well of you".

### 5. Expressive and interactive: 6/10

The big change since review 1 is that you can talk back, and the responses have voice: Juniper to a disagreement, "The light has gone a little grey"; Marlow, "That's a bit hurtful, honestly"; Ada, "I would rather an honest disagreement than a kind evasion"; Fen to a second sorry, "Enough sorry. Do better."; Juniper to a sorry, "The splinter works loose a little." That is the bar's "see it land", and it earns the point up. But the replies are not keyed to what was just said. Across 102 answers "I'm sorry" was offered after 53, and in the first 27 the pattern was clear: seven followed outright praise ("I could not ask for a better steward", "You are the kind of weather we needed", "Marlow! Love Marlow!"), four followed a shrug ("No view on the well"), none followed a complaint. Taking it got "Then let us put it behind us", "Ha! Well! About time! Hug it out!", "I will try to let it go", and five days later Wren recalled "you and I made it up", when nothing had gone wrong. The day after I felled the old oak only Ada ("I shall miss the old oak more than I expected") and Wren ("It looks so empty where the old oak was") reacted; when I apologised to all ten residents on day 13 every one offered the chip and not one mentioned the tree: Bram, "Sorry sorry sorry! Enough!"; Marlow, "Sorry again? Let it be, now."; Wren, "You don't need to keep saying sorry. Just... Show me." The answers are still stitched: "I need a good long sleep, more than most." closed 8 of 18 "What's on your mind?" answers; "I work at the …" closed 8 of 18 "How are you?" answers; Ada recited her full bio ("I taught school for forty years…") inside "What do you think of me?" in three separate sessions; her day-10 mind answer was seven unrelated sentences ("I have made my request. Now I must be patient. Today has been most agreeable. I am grateful to you. I should have said so sooner. I have not forgotten that you listened… I sleep lightly… I am happiest at home, truth be told."). In the 30-day radio run "Ask me how I am! Wonderful, that's how!" appeared 12 times and "What a day! What a town!" seven.

## Verdict

**Does not meet the bar.** Highest count 7, pass mark 8 on all five. Talking back and a town that can lose someone are both in, and both are the right shape. What still holds it under: the four replies fire whether or not they fit the sentence before them; the answers are lists of facts rather than a person speaking; nothing that happens to the town, an empty larder included, reaches how anyone feels; the board fills with parked asks rather than events; and a handful of on-screen faults (mud slab, clipped widget, my words in their bubble, a modal in the first twenty seconds) read as unfinished to anyone who looks.

## Problems, ranked by how much they hold the game back

### 1. Replies ignore what was just said

Evidence: "I'm sorry" offered after 53 of 102 answers, including all 10 of 10 "What do you think of me?" answers on day 13 when 8 of them had just praised me; in the first 27 answers it followed praise 7 times, a shrug 4 times and a complaint never; "Let me explain" offered to Ada and Fen on day 3 right after "I could not ask for a better steward" and "Steward listens. Rare thing."; Bram's "Ha! Well! About time! Hug it out!" to a sorry he had no reason to receive; Wren's "you and I made it up, five days ago" born from a sorry after "I don't really know the garden plots well enough"; a sorry for the oak the day after it fell got "Sorry sorry sorry! Enough!" because the earlier unneeded sorries had used up `SORRY_GAP`; "That's fair" the only reply to "The well is a blank page to me". `offersFor` in `src/sim/replies.ts` opens sorry on any held grievance regardless of the answer's band or subject.
Fix: offer sorry and explain only when the answer just given (its memory, topic or `because`) is the grievance, and make agree and disagree name the claim they answer.

### 2. Answers are stitched lists, and contradict themselves

Evidence: "I need a good long sleep, more than most." on 8 of 18 mind answers; "I work at the …" on 8 of 18 how answers; "X is my closest friend here" / "I haven't found a favourite spot yet" bolted to opinion answers; Ada's seven-sentence day-10 mind answer; the bio recited inside "What do you think of me?" in three sessions; same-breath contradictions ("I am grateful to you… Frankly, you still irritate me", day 1; "rather good spirits" vs "you have failed us" on neglected day 22; "Patience." as a one-word sentence on the phone). `reconcile` in `src/sim/talk.ts` only strips topics by band; the facts, bio and friend lines are added after it.
Fix: pick one fact only when it answers the question, never the bio, and run the same-subject, opposite-valence dedupe over the whole answer including facts and memories.

### 3. Nothing that happens to the town reaches how anyone feels

Evidence: engaged food at 0.4 to 1.0 on days 24, 25 and 28 with ten residents, mean mood 0.77 to 0.79 every one of those nights and no resident under 0.63; one log line ("Juniper makes do with a meagre meal") and then twelve near-identical asks on day 29; 21 nights of neglect with mean mood 0.67 to 0.74, the day-14 board the day-1 board plus three dream asks, and the first consequence a player sees is "Bram has left" on day 20 with Ada saying "rather good spirits" two days later; after a week of empty larder six of ten "think the world of you".
Fix: let hunger, unmet asks and standing pull mood and the "How are you?" band down inside a week, make a bare larder a scene (a thin supper, a quarrel over it, someone skipping work), show someone packing before they go, and add a soak check that the larder-empty week moves mean mood by at least 0.1.

### 4. The board fills with parked asks, not things to decide

Evidence: days 6 to 28 of the engaged board were the same dream cards until built; 13 of the last 14 mornings in `considerate` and 9 of the last 11 in `none` had zero asks; 3 proposal types, 4 proposals in 30 days; the contraption re-posed 16 times across days 7 to 10 and 22 to 24 and never lapsed though the modal promises two days; dreams wait on the steward for weeks ("wait for the orchard to be planted" days 5 to 14, "watch the trade cart come and go" days 2 to 9, the glasshouse asked on days 14 and 21). Hamlet at 44 renown on day 2.
Fix: a daily prompt drawn from live state (a quarrel to settle, two residents wanting one plot, a newcomer whose trade has no building), six or more proposal types with three options, residents who take their own next step after three days, a lapse timer that "Decide later" does not reset, and a tier curve that is not done in two mornings.

### 5. On-screen faults a player will screenshot

Evidence: brown trampled slab over the centre by day 5 (d05), the whole middle by day 12 (d12), a mud rectangle in the snow (n21, n22); Goals widget clipping the tier card ("Hamlet" half visible, d05; "it opens a chicken", d12); Wren's bubble carrying my words "I don't see it that way." (d05); quick card "Now: socialize at the old oak until …" and a bubble cut at "grow old…" (p07); a proposal modal over the town 20 real seconds into a fresh phone game (p03b); "0 of 0 who wished for it have it: Bram." (neglected day 21 board); "▲ Day 29: let the larder run bare; said sorry" and "▲ Day 20: kept me waiting; let Marlow's market day quietly drop" (d30-you, n22-you); Juniper's bio shown twice on her own page and Abel's semicolon-joined fact (d30-about).
Fix: clamp wear to laid paths and tracks, let the Goals widget grow or scroll, give bubbles only the resident's half of a reply line, size bubbles and the quick card to their text, hold the first proposal until the player has opened one page, drop a wish when its last wisher leaves, never merge opposite-sign ledger entries, and dedupe the bio out of the fact list.

### 6. Approval is instant, cheap and forgiving

Evidence: "I could not ask for a better steward" on day 3 after three 1-timber plants and a bakery; Bram +0.82 and Wren +0.83 by day 9; Juniper −0.48 on day 17 to +0.54 on day 30 with her proposal still unanswered; 6 of 10 "think the world of you" on day 30 after the larder week; Bertie arriving into the neglected town on the day Bram left and "thinks well of you"; Marlow at +0.16 "Happy to"; 16 of 17 favours a yes or "still on the last thing"; timber 13 to 35 to 47 by day 8, 59 unspent on day 30.
Fix: cap standing gain per day and let it decay toward neutral, start newcomers neutral and let the town's talk set them, refuse favours on low standing or low mood more often than once in seventeen, and shrink the Hamlet gift.

### 7. The log repeats itself

Evidence: "Ask me how I am! Wonderful, that's how!" 12 times, "What a day! What a town!" 7, "Everything is brilliant today!" 7, "Everything feels a bit golden today." 7 in the 30-day `favours` run; "I sent my wish off. I wonder where it went." 9 times in 21 days of `none`; "I'm happy today. Properly happy." 5 and "I have not had a proper conversation in days." 5 in `considerate`; "Rain's coming down. Good day for a cup of tea." twice from Clem in one day-30 afternoon.
Fix: a per-town, per-line cooldown of three days and at least five lines per thought.

### 8. Reasons shown to the player are generic

Evidence: the neglected You tab is "kept me waiting" on 19 of 27 lines and "nothing was done" on 7; "Kit thinks less of the steward: built the workshop." with no why; "Juniper asks for help with a dream: a granary. It matters a great deal to them." for every dream card; "Settle into the valley / Get to know the neighbours" for every newcomer.
Fix: write the ledger line from the ask that lapsed ("waited eleven days for green by my door"), let the dream card quote the resident, and give each newcomer trade its own first dream.

### 9. Links with a seed never restore

Evidence: any URL with `scenario` or `seed` starts a fresh town on every load (`web/src/main.ts` line 22), silently, with the intro skipped by `intro=0`; a shared link is a game you cannot come back to.
Fix: restore a save that matches the URL's scenario and seed, and only start fresh on `?new=1`.
